# Codex Micro Glass Guide — three-minute GitHub narration

This is **Codex Micro Glass Guide**.

I have a very cute, and not inexpensive, device on my desk: the Work Louder and OpenAI Codex Micro. It was the first limited release of a functional Codex product. The demonstration videos make it look almost magical. People fly through their work with a dial, joystick, glowing Agent keys, and six programmable layers.

I thought, yes. I am going to use this for everything.

Then I sat down in front of mine and forgot what the buttons did.

That is the problem with programmable hardware. Seeing what it can do is not the same as remembering how to do it. One button can mean something completely different on another layer. If I have to stop, search the documentation, and rebuild the map in my head every time, the friction wins.

And I did not buy a lovely, expensive tool so it could become a dust-gathering paperweight.

I am a visual learner. I needed the controls to stay visible while I worked. I needed color, position, and plain-language reminders that I could understand at a glance.

So I built **Codex Micro Glass Guide**.

It is a translucent Mac guide that floats above my other apps while I learn the device. I can move it, resize it, and click a control for a simple reminder without leaving my work.

The actual layout is right there: the white dial, joystick, touch sensor, layer lights, command buttons, and six glowing Agent keys. A quick touch cycles through the six layers. The three small lights show which layer is active. My labels can match the shortcuts I really use.

I think of it as training wheels made of glass. The guide stays beside me while repetition becomes familiarity. The point is not to keep reading the guide forever. The point is to use the hardware until my hands know what to do.

The public project includes the Codex skill and a privacy-safe Mac app template. Personal task names and links stay in your local copy.

And this is not one perfect layout for everyone. Rename your layers. Change the colors. Rewrite the notes. Adapt it for your hands, your brain, your workflow, and your desk. Then share the privacy-safe ideas that could help someone else.

I built **Codex Micro Glass Guide** to turn an exciting piece of hardware into a tool I will actually use.

The skill and source are in the repository. Make it yours, build the muscle memory, and do not let your Codex Micro gather dust.
